01 / PREMISE

The past changes.
The traveller doesn't.

Foreshadow is built around one central consequence: when a traveller alters the past, the world rewrites itself — but the traveller retains the memories of the timeline that no longer exists.

The further back the change is made, the more distant the returning world can become. A small intervention may produce a few unsettling discrepancies. A larger one can return the traveller to relationships, histories and choices that feel emotionally real to everyone except them.

Time travel is not only a plot device. It is cumulative psychological damage.

02 / THE RULES

01

Only the traveller remembers

Everyone else belongs completely to the revised timeline. There is no shared proof that the previous version ever happened.

02

Distance magnifies consequence

The further back someone travels, the greater the possible divergence when they return.

03

Memory accumulates

A traveller can carry two, three or four incompatible lives in one mind. None of those memories feel imaginary.

04

The method has limits

The original plant or ingredient may permit only short jumps. Extending its reach requires increasingly aggressive refinement.

05

Every fix creates a new present

The question is never simply “did it work?” It is “what did success cost elsewhere?”

06

No premonitions

The film stays focused on altered memory, consequence and identity rather than adding a second supernatural mechanism.

The title Foreshadow can become part of the film's machinery: small details in the current timeline quietly reveal the shape of changes that have already happened to it.

04 / STORY ENGINE

1

Discovery

A natural substance allows a limited displacement through time. The first use feels extraordinary but containable.

2

Need

A reason emerges to go further. The original method is not strong enough.

3

Refinement

Jay and Ade push the substance harder. Improving the process begins to resemble dependency.

4

Divergence

Each successful jump returns them to a present that is harder to recognise and psychologically harder to hold.

5

Identity

The final problem is no longer merely how to fix history, but which history they have the right to restore.
